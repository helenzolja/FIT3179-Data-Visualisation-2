Exploring Australian Native Plants

Diversity of Australian Plants

Australia has over 20'000 native vascular plant species, which account for roughly 10% of the global total, with almost 9 in 10 Australian plant species (88%) being endemic, meaning they are found nowhere else on Earth.

Within the large range of unique species, the country's flora is mainly dominated by several major taxonomic plant groups that have adapted to Australia's drought and fire-prone landscape.

As visualised on the right, flowering plants or angiosperms make up the vast majority of the continent's vascular flora and dominate the world's ecosystems. Minor plant groups such as gymnosperms consist of conifers, cycads and their relatives. Bryophytes, consisting of mosses, liverworts and hornworts, are small, non-vascular, spore-producing plants that play a role in soil stabilisation and other ecological processes.

Although worldwide species totals are much larger, comparing the groups reveals how Australia's contribution varies across different branches of plant diversity.

Flora Distribution by State

Source: Australian flora statistics (1992).

As observed from the flora distribution map, Queensland (QLD) and Western Australia (WA) exhibit half of Australia's native plants, while naturalised or introduced species cluster in the south-east around New South Wales (NSW). In comparison, Tasmania (TAS) contain the smaller percentage of flora, both native or naturalised.

Since QLD and WA coast a diverse range of climates, more plant species are likely to grow there, supported by its vast isolated landscapes with less human interference. In contrast, TAS has a cooler climate and is geographically seperated from the mainland, reducing chances of plant species to disperse and establish there. NSW has a higher percentage of naturalised plants as it was settled first, coasting a long history of European settlement, ports and agriculture that allowed for the introduction of non-native species.

Where Were Plants Recorded in 2025?

Across Australia, 954,803 plant records were made in 2025, but they are far from evenly spread. The tallest spikes rise over Sydney (56,067 records), Melbourne (54,809) and Sydney's west and the Blue Mountains (41,670), while half of all squares have 69 records or fewer.

Records follow people: most are made near cities and along the east coast, which is worth remembering when reading the maps of plants that follow.

Where Are Victoria's Native Plants Observed?

In 2025, people made 80,613 observations of native plants across Victoria. They are far from evenly spread: about 72% fall in just 1 in 10 squares. The busiest square, in the Dandenong Ranges east of Melbourne, has 3,732 observations on its own.

The darkest squares sit around national parks and botanic gardens, such as the Dandenong Ranges, the Grampians, Croajingolong and the Royal Botanic Gardens: places people visit to see native plants.

Note: Each square is about 10 km across, so every square covers the same amount of land and colours can be compared fairly. Observations follow where people walk and look for plants. Park and garden symbols mark approximate centres. Observations with deliberately blurred locations (usually threatened plants) are not shown. Hover over a square for its exact count.

Which Native Plants Take Over Each Season?

Gum trees and wattles are the most recorded native genera for most of the year, but the seasons reshuffle the rest. Spider orchids (Caladenia) rank last from January to July, then climb to 2nd in September as they flower in spring. In Western Australia they reach 1st in September and October, while sundews lead there in winter.
