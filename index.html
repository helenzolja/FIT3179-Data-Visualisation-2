Exploring Australian Native Plants

Diversity of Australian Plants

Australia has over 20'000 native vascular plant species, which account for roughly 10% of the global total, with almost 9 in 10 Australian plant species (88%) being endemic, meaning they are found nowhere else on Earth.

Within the large range of unique species, the country's flora is mainly dominated by several major taxonomic plant groups that have adapted to Australia's drought and fire-prone landscape.

As visualised on the right, flowering plants or angiosperms make up the vast majority of the continent's vascular flora and dominate the world's ecosystems. Minor plant groups such as gymnosperms consist of conifers, cycads and their relatives. Bryophytes, consisting of mosses, liverworts and hornworts, are small, non-vascular, spore-producing plants that play a role in soil stabilisation and other ecological processes.

Flora Distribution by State

Source: Australian flora statistics (1992).

As observed from the flora distribution map, Queensland (QLD) and Western Australia (WA) exhibit half of Australia's native plants, while naturalised or introduced species cluster in the south-east around New South Wales (NSW). In comparison, Tasmania (TAS) contain the smaller percentage of flora, both native or naturalised.

Since QLD and WA coast a diverse range of climates, more plant species are likely to grow there, supported by its vast isolated landscapes with less human interference. In contrast, TAS has a cooler climate and is geographically seperated from the mainland, reducing chances of plant species to disperse and establish there. NSW has a higher percentage of naturalised plants as it was settled first, coasting a long history of European settlement, ports and agriculture that allowed for the introduction of non-native species.
