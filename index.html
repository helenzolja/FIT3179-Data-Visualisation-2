Exploring Australian Native Plants

How does Australia's flora compare with the world, where is it found and recorded, when does it appear, and what threatens it?

1

Diversity of Australian Plants

Australia has over 20,000 native vascular plant species, roughly 10% of the global total, and almost 9 in 10 of them (88%) are endemic: found nowhere else on Earth.

Flowering plants (angiosperms) make up the vast majority of the continent's flora. Smaller groups include gymnosperms (conifers, cycads and their relatives) and bryophytes (mosses, liverworts and hornworts), small spore-producing plants that help stabilise soil.

Australia holds about 7% of the world's described flowering plants, but more than a quarter of its described plant algae.

Source: ANBG, Australian flora statistics (1992).

Queensland and Western Australia each contain almost half (48%) of Australia's native species. Tasmania has the smallest share, at 10%.

Switch to naturalised plants (species introduced from overseas that now grow wild) and the pattern moves south-east: New South Wales holds 64% of them and Queensland 59%, while the Northern Territory has only 13%.

One likely reason is that large states span many climates, while the south-east has the longest history of European settlement, ports and farming, which are common routes for introduced plants.
